## Measurements and bound selected before concurrency repair

The [read-only local observation](refresh/offline-observation.json) found two offline peers. Completion requests took 0.5353 s and 16.7808 s; folder status took 0.0151 s. The latter completion exceeds the 10 s refresh interval, confirming an offline-peer overrun in this observation. Neither request reached the 30 s timeout: that specific timeout hypothesis remains unconfirmed. No configuration, peer connectivity or data was changed. Only endpoint families, durations and anonymous ordinal/count information are retained.

The deterministic fixture establishes head-of-line blocking independently of that diagnosis. Select **two concurrent requests per entry kind**: at most four expensive requests, plus version and connections (six monitoring requests total). Refill each completed slot immediately. This is the smallest cap allowing fast entries past one held request, and avoids unbounded daemon load. Preserve the existing default URLSession 10 s inactivity / 30 s resource limits; do not change polling cadence. With every earlier slot stalled, entry admission is bounded by preceding waves of at most 30 s per two entries, plus scheduler overhead. This is an admission bound under production URLSession deadlines, not a claim of constant latency regardless of folder/device count. An injected transport must honor cancellation/deadlines to provide that wall-clock guarantee; controlled tests explicitly release every held response.

## Implementation and independent review

- The client owns monitoring timers, active-pass cancellation and one pending follow-up. Ordinary triggers coalesce; connection edits invalidate immediately and prepare latest credentials after old work unwinds. A manual waiter completes with its requested pass, even if later timer work remains queued.
- Every monitoring success/failure checks current generation before changing published state, caches, history, errors or notifications. Timer revisions reject queued obsolete callbacks. Shutdown prevents restart. Demo entry/exit invalidates real work; settings edits preserve the complete synthetic presentation.
- Bounded workers refill independently, and an entry failure invalidates only its own status. No stale-universe folder/device requests follow a configuration failure.
- Global completion tracking moved from icon rendering into the client's completed-pass boundary. Continuous queued work cannot suppress completion forever; the existing Refresh control remains enabled and queues a follow-up. Genuine connection failure resets both global and per-folder completion history.
- Independent review identified partially cleared demo metrics and completion starvation during continuous polling. Both were reproduced before repair (two tests, five failed assertions), fixed and reviewed again. An additional failing regression caught the connection-outage history gap. Final independent review reported no remaining blocking concurrency/lifecycle/publication findings; review itself did not execute tests.
